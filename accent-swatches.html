An interactive accent for the portfolio

The site has one accent and it means “live” — it is only used by the tournament UI. Nothing carries a shared colour for “this responds to you.” This page puts one candidate blue on the real paper, on real components, with every contrast ratio measured in the browser rather than asserted.

Every number on this page is computed at runtime from the actual rendered colours (WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula). Thresholds shown: 4.5 for body-size text, 3.0 for large text, focus rings and other non-text UI.

1 · The candidate and its family

One base, one pressed step, one wash, one dark-ground variant. Everything is the same hue (262°) so the family reads as one colour at different energies.

2 · Against the two references

Apple’s system blue and the blue on the reference case study, shown as body-size link text on the real paper. Both are lighter than ours; both miss 4.5:1. That failure is the reason ours is darker, not a stylistic preference.

Focus rings · today’s ink vs accent

The 1.00:1 looks alarming and isn’t a failure: every ring on the site uses a positive outline-offset, so a band of paper always separates ring from control, and paper is the adjacent colour that counts. Both rings are legal. The accent ring would trade 18.42:1 for 5.59:1 across ~60 selectors — which is why the spec says keep ink.

4 · “Live” and “interactive” side by side

Green stays the broadcast signal. It only ever appears as a fill, border or dot on a card. Blue only ever appears as text, an underline or an outline on something you can point at. Different hue, different geometry, different place on the page.

The last number is the honest caveat: blue and green are almost the same lightness, so they separate by hue only. Never place them adjacent as two text colours, and never let either state be signalled by colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1) — the live card already carries a dot and a “NOW” label, and the interactive state always carries a hover, an underline or a focus outline.

5 · Dark ground (the Play sky)

The base blue is a paper colour. On a night sky it collapses. A lighter same-hue variant is required — this is the same move Apple makes going from #007AFF to #0A84FF.
