Token specimen old   vs  new  —

What this page is — read this first

This is a proposal from 2026-08-02, not a picture of the shipped system. It is standalone by design — it links no stylesheet and copies the tokens it demonstrates into its own :root. Measured 2026-08-09: it declares 70 custom properties against the 316 that ship in tokens.css + controls.css, so it covers 20.6% of the system, and the copies can drift without anything reporting it — --lh-prose already did (it asserted 1.55 for a week after 1.5 shipped; corrected above).

It shows none of: the leading ladder (--lh-tight/head/note/prose), the control layer (--ctl-*, 44/36px rungs, the eleven .ctl variants, .field, .ctl--range, .ctl--swatch), the surface primitives (.surface, .collection, .media), the six-rung motion ladder (--dur-press → --dur-enter) or the fixed --sp-* spacing ladder. Those are the parts of the system a reviewer would actually ask about.

The shipped system is tokens.css and controls.css. When those two and this page disagree, they are right and this page is stale. Conformance measured against them: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-09-system-conformance-audit.md.

How to read this page

Every block below is a pair. Left is what the site does today. Right is what the token spec proposes. The red/green tag in each header tells you which is which; on a narrow screen they stack, old on top.

The typeface is Instrument Sans on both sides. It is not changing — §5 of the spec is a leading, tracking and spacing pass on the face already in use. Nothing needs downloading.

3. Corner smoothing — corner-shape: squircle

border-radius is a circular arc: curvature jumps where the arc meets the straight edge. Apple's corner is curvature-continuous. Chrome/Edge 139+ ship corner-shape; everywhere else the declaration is dropped and the border-radius already there is used. Nothing to guard, nothing to polyfill. The third pane overlays the two outlines so the difference is unmissable.

Accessibility trap, for the record: the cross-browser fallback is clip-path, and clip-path clips box-shadow and the focus ring as well as the content. 4. Buttons — 4px → 14px, and a spring on press

Twenty-one selectors currently sit at 4px: .talk .back .csTab .hmBtn .moodBtn .aboutCta .ndLink and the rest. All become --r-md 14px. Press one — the old side eases, the new side is critically damped so a fast double-tap can't stack a wobble.

OLD 4px · ease-out 160ms 1px hard rim

Height 44px, radius 4px — a ratio of 0.09. Reads as a form control.

NEW 14px · --sp-quick 300ms --rim-1 hairline

Radius 14 on a 44px button = 0.32. Apple's nonscrolling button is 22pt on ~50pt = 0.44, so this sits just inside it — correct for an editorial site. The last one is --r-pill, for when the shape is the affordance.

8. Motion — sampled springs, and the one bezier that works

Each curve is a real damped-oscillator response sampled into CSS linear(). linear() is shape only, so each is paired with the duration at which its physics is real. The existing house curve cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) stays for anything under 200ms.

9. The header — why it falls flat on sub-pages

Verified in the source, not assumed. The home page and the case studies do not share a header component — they share a stylesheet.

index.html: nav{display:flex;align-items:center;gap:var(--sp-16-24)} carrying burger, drawer, .navTitle, .heroBack, .navGroup.navL (Work / About me), the logo, .navGroup.navR (the whole Play menu) and #talk.
All five case studies: nav{display:grid;grid-template-columns:1fr auto 1fr} with exactly three children — .back.backlink, .logo, .talk. I confirmed each of the five carries the full .navLink and .moodBtn CSS (4 and 8 occurrences per file) and zero matching markup. Styles travelled; structure didn't.

So it is not a reduced header, it is a different, sparser component — and a 1fr/auto/1fr grid with one item per cell has no way to look like a bar. That is the thing to fix, and it is a structural fix, not a styling one.

Token-layer gap this exposed, and how I closed it. Every treatment below needed a horizontal inset for a control inside a bar — the distance from the pill's edge to the first item's text. Nothing in the spec covered it, and inventing it per-treatment is exactly how 60 one-off radii happen. Added to §7 of the spec: --pad-bar:5px (the pill's own padding, which sets the concentric inner radius) and --gap-bar:2px (between items in a group). With --r-pill outside and 5px of padding, items take --r-pill too — a pill inside a pill is the one case where the concentric rule says equal, because both ends are already fully round.

10. The "Jayden Betts" lockup — caps, retuned caps, or sentence case

Every number in the current mark is derived from the caps setting, so dropping text-transform:uppercase invalidates all three of them at once. I measured each against the actual instrument-sans-variable.woff2 rather than guessing. Weight stays 600 in all three — the two-weight rule allows nothing between, and 400 goes weedy at 17px.

What I measured. Glyph metrics from instrument-sans-variable.woff2 (fontTools): cap height 0.720 em, x-height 0.510, y descender −0.205, n advance 1.175× its own x-height.

Width matching was re-solved in the browser, not from the font tables — the face carries an avar table that remaps the wdth axis non-linearly (−0.48 → −0.50), so advance sums taken from an instanced font disagree with what Chrome actually renders by up to 2%. Binary search on live getBoundingClientRect() is the only trustworthy number here:
→ Caps @ .08em width-match at 85.5%. The shipped 85.8% is 0.4% out — effectively correct. Credit where due; that value was well chosen.
→ Caps @ .10em want 85.0%.
→ Sentence case stacked wants 77.75% — 7.75pp deeper than caps, and only 2.75pp above the axis floor of 75%. That is the finding that decides this.

Tracking: .08em was a capitals value — tall narrow caps need opening. Lowercase does not, and Instrument Sans is already loosely spaced for its x-height (n advance = 1.175× x-height, vs ~1.08 for a face like Inter). So the sign flips: −.008em, not +.10em.

font-stretch: gone entirely. One line means there is nothing to width-match, so the mark stops depending on a variable width axis — which §8 of the spec flags as its most fragile dependency.

Leading: the stacked version can't keep 1.04. Sentence case puts a y descender on line 1, so the ink extent goes 0.730 em → 0.925 em and the visible gap collapses from 0.310 em to 0.115 em. Holding the old gap needs line-height 1.235 — at which point the stack is taller than it is compact, and the reason for stacking is gone.

Why one line wins, in one sentence: the stack existed to make a caps mark feel compact, and sentence case removes both the need and the means — the width-match it depends on would take "Jayden" to 77.75% against "Betts" at 100% — 2.75pp off the axis floor — and at that depth the lowercase bowls and stems visibly stop matching, which caps at 85.5% got away with. One line at 17px measures 100.0px × 17.8px against the stacked mark's 61.4px × 34.0px: wider, half the height, and three declarations lighter (text-transform, font-stretch, and the stacked spans all go). Premium = subtract.

11. Leading — one law, anchored on the hero

Five --lh-* tokens exist and are used five times in total (--lh-body and --lh-flat: zero). Meanwhile 22 distinct raw line-height values are set inline. Leading is less tokenised than radius.

The fix is a curve, not a constant: lh(s) = 0.909 + 10.25/s, fitted through the hero's existing 1.08 at 60px and body at 1.55 at 16px. Because em in line-height resolves against the element's own font-size, it is one declaration that tracks every clamp() automatically: --lh-auto: clamp(0.95em, 0.91em + 10.25px, 1.6em). The hero is not exempt from the ladder — it defines it.

12. Tracking — everything closes up, small caps open up

Measured off the woff2: Instrument Sans has an 0.72 em cap height (tall), an 0.51 em x-height (low), and an n advance of 1.175× its x-height — versus ~1.08 for a face like Inter. It is spaced for UI. That means it runs slack at display sizes and its tall caps run cramped at label sizes, so the ladder is steeper at both ends than generic advice.

13. Space around headings — where premium typography actually lives

Page gaps stay on the --sp-* ladder; they are about the page. Heading gaps go em-relative to the heading, because a heading's correct margins are a function of its own size — and every heading here is a clamp(), so a fixed px margin is wrong at one end of every one. --gap-head-top 1.6em over --gap-head-bot .42em is a 3.8:1 ratio, and the ratio is the whole point.
