## The thing that shapes everything else

There are two hiring systems, and you have fallen into the gap between them.

**Cohort postings** (New Grad, University Grad, College Grad) gate on **graduation year**. Right now
they are recruiting the **class of 2027**. Palantir says it outright. Solace's title says it. As a
June 2026 graduate you are one cycle late — those postings opened around August–October 2025 and
you were still in school for the next one.

**Standing requisitions** gate on **years of experience**, not graduation year. They mostly start
at 3+ years. A few start at 2.

So the good news and the bad news are the same fact: **the job you have been doing since April 2026
is what pushes you out of the cohort lane and into the experience lane.** Head of User Experience &
Product Design at R3SHORE is real professional design work. It is not two years of it yet, but it
is not zero, and it is the thing that makes a 2-year requisition worth an application instead of a
fantasy.

Practical consequence: **do not present yourself as a new grad by default.** For cohort roles you
are a recent graduate. For everything else you are a working product designer with a shipped brand,
a design system, and a startup's entire UX on your name. Those are two different cover letters.

**That is the whole bar.** I swept all 37 open roles in Apple's US Human Interface Design
organisation and read every minimum-qualifications list. The next-lowest product design bar is
4+ years (Creator Studio); most are 5, 7 or 8. This one asks for pictures of your work.

**Why it suits you specifically.** The description is unusually direct, and worth quoting properly:

> *"We do not want to hire more people like the people already on the team. We are looking for
> someone to add something special. **We are open to people with any background and any amount of
> experience.** It does not matter to us what tool you use or what medium you work in. We are
> interested in your ideas and how you express them."*

Then it lists what they want, and you have shipped evidence of nearly all of it:

- *"**Prototyping.** Make something people can try with taps, swipes, or clicks. Not necessarily an app, whatever communicates an idea."* — Strata is playable on your own site, with sound. Not a video of a prototype. The prototype.
- *"**Animation.** Use motion in a complementary and tasteful way to direct attention and describe interactions… a balance between artful *how it feels*, and practical *what it communicates*."* — the tower dance, the whole motion layer of your portfolio.
- *"**Interaction.** Design new affordances and capabilities that are discoverable, easy to perform, and provide clear feedback."* — the Apple Wallet Member ID in the UC Davis Rec redesign is exactly this: a new affordance built out of an existing behaviour.
- *"**Initiative.** Ideas and concepts no one asked for. You just jump in and make things. You do not wait for instructions or need permission."* — the greeting-card company at ten, the hundred-page comic, Strata because the assignment stopped at a pitch, the Rec redesign because the app annoyed you. Your About page is a four-paragraph answer to this bullet.

**What might disqualify you.** Three honest things. **It is Apple**, so "no formal bar" means the

salary is listed, so you would be negotiating blind.

**How to apply.** They ask for pictures or videos. Do not send a résumé and a link and hope.
Send the playable Strata build, the tower-dance walkthrough, and the Wallet interaction — the three

## What the market actually looks like right now

I did not sample. I queried the machine-readable job-board APIs behind **212 company career sites**
and read **roughly 14,500 live postings** — every Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby board I could reach
across consumer tech, fintech, AI labs, dev tools, health and social. On top of that I went through
Apple's, Amazon's, Google's, Meta's and TikTok's own hiring systems directly, including reading
every one of the 37 minimum-qualification lists in Apple's US Human Interface Design organisation.

Junior or new-grad product design roles in that entire sweep: **five.** All five are Palantir's, and
all five require a 2027 graduation date.

That number is the most useful thing in this document. **The reason you are not getting responses is
not your portfolio.** There is close to nothing at your level currently posted in the startup and
mid-cap tech ecosystem, and the two biggest cohort programmes for designers — Google's and Meta's —
have no design roles open at all today. This is a timing problem and a market problem before it is
a you problem.

Two things follow from that:

1. **Cohort season is about to start.** University and new-grad postings for the class of 2027
   cluster in **late August through October**. That window is opening in the next few weeks. Those
   will mostly be gated to 2027 graduates and mostly closed to you — but historically some
   companies post both a 2027 cohort role *and* an ungated junior req at the same time, because
   that is when they get budget. **Check weekly from now through October.** This is the single
   highest-value habit available to you between now and then.
2. **Your leverage is the experience lane, not the cohort lane.** Every month you stay at R3SHORE,
   the 2-year requisitions get more reachable and the cohort roles get less relevant. By roughly
   spring 2027 you clear Discord's stated concession honestly rather than arguing past it.

## What your materials are missing for *these specific* applications

Your case studies are strong and I am not going to relitigate the shipped-outcome-metrics question —
that is already handled in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-09-copy-options.md`. These are only the
gaps that bite on the applications above.

**1. Your site and your résumé disagree about where you live.** The résumé header says
`Davis, CA 95616`. About says *"I am a San Francisco-based product designer"* and
*"Based in — San Francisco, CA · open to relocating."* Recruiters and ATS filters read both. For
Bay Area roles the SF framing helps you; the mismatch is what hurts, because it reads as
carelessness on the one document where carelessness is fatal. **Pick one and make both say it.**
Given Solace is Redwood City and TikTok is San Jose, "San Francisco Bay Area · open to relocating"
is defensible on both.

**2. Your site and your résumé disagree about what you do.** The résumé summary says
*"specializing in iOS, B2B, and design systems."* About says *"I focus on iOS, B2C, and design
systems."* **B2B and B2C are opposites**, and this is the first line a reviewer reads on each
document. Every role in Tier 1 is consumer. Fix the résumé to B2C.

**3. Nothing in your materials says "AI product design" in those words.** Both TikTok roles ask for
it explicitly — one in the minimum quals, one in the preferred. You have the substance already:
North's design rationale in Apollo, and the whole "AI is a tool, not a replacement" section of
Strata, which is a genuinely thoughtful position most applicants cannot articulate. But a recruiter
scanning your résumé will not find the phrase. **Add one line to the Apollo bullet naming North as
AI product design, and add "AI-assisted prototyping" or similar to your skills row.** That is a
fifteen-minute change with disproportionate return on the two best-fit roles you have.

**4. Two of your five case studies are invisible on your résumé.** It covers R3SHORE, the Design
Interactive cohort (Bearings), and the fellowship — and Cluster appears only obliquely, as *"a mobile

gets screened first. **Add a one-line Projects block.** Strata — built end to end in Lovable,

**5. Your title is going to raise an eyebrow, and that is fine — prepare the sentence.**
"Head of User Experience & Product Design" on a four-month tenure at an early-stage company is

**6. Portfolio password.** Palantir's postings ask for it explicitly if any part is gated. Yours is

## Where these roles are actually posted — a method you can repeat

The list above goes stale. This does not.

### The technique that beat every job board

Most companies run their careers page on one of three applicant tracking systems, and **all three
expose a public JSON endpoint listing every genuinely live role.** No login, no scraping, no stale
aggregator data. This is how I swept 212 companies, and you can run it yourself in a terminal:

```bash
# Greenhouse  (Figma, Discord, Chime, Reddit, Airbnb, Robinhood, Stripe, Gusto, Klaviyo …)
curl -s "https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/discord/jobs" | grep -i design

# Lever       (Palantir, Match Group, Gopuff …)
curl -s "https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/palantir?mode=json" | grep -i design

# Ashby       (Solace, OpenAI, Notion, Ramp, Linear, Perplexity, Cursor …)
curl -s "https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/solace" | grep -i design
```

The company token is usually just the company name lowercased. Add `?content=true` on Greenhouse to
pull full descriptions and grep for `years` — that is how you filter out the 3+ roles in seconds
instead of opening forty tabs. If a company's careers page URL contains `greenhouse.io`,
`lever.co` or `ashbyhq.com`, this works on it.

Three of the big names have their own open endpoints, and **Apple's is the most valuable thing in
this document after the shortlist itself** — it is how I found the Prototyping Designer role:

```bash
# Apple — full job detail including minimum qualifications, by role number
curl -s "https://jobs.apple.com/api/v1/jobDetails/200631554?locale=en-us"

# Apple — search a whole design sub-team (POST). Swap subTeam for other teams.
curl -s -X POST "https://jobs.apple.com/api/v1/search" -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"query":"","filters":{"postingpostLocation":["postLocation-USA"],
       "teams":[{"team":"teamsAndSubTeams-DESGN","subTeam":"subTeam-HID"}]},
       "page":1,"locale":"en-us","sort":"newest",
       "format":{"longDate":"MMMM D, YYYY","mediumDate":"MMM D, YYYY"}}'

# Amazon — returns JSON, sortable by recency. This is how I found the Amazon Music role.
curl -s "https://www.amazon.jobs/en/search.json?base_query=product+designer&country=USA&result_limit=50&sort=recent"

# Google — filter the careers URL to early career and read the result count
# https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/?q=designer&target_level=EARLY
```

Workday powers most large non-tech employers (Target, Mastercard, Disney and similar). Its endpoint
is a POST — `https://<company>.wd{1,3,5}.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/<company>/<site>/jobs` with a
body of `{"appliedFacets":{},"limit":20,"offset":0,"searchText":"designer"}`. A 404 mentioning
`Job_Posting_Site_ID` means the tenant is right but the site name is wrong; a 422 means the host is
wrong. Fiddly, but it works.

**Why this matters more than the list:** it tells you what is live *right now*, straight from the
system the recruiter uses. Aggregators lag by weeks. That TikTok 404 is what lag looks like.

### The one habit that matters most

**Titles lie about level. Minimum qualifications do not.** Every genuinely reachable role in this
document was found by reading the requirements, not by filtering on a title:

- "Prototyping Designer" at **Apple** sounds senior and asks for pictures of your work.
- "Product Designer" at **Chime** sounds junior and pays $245,000 for extensive experience.
- "Associate Product Designer" at **Solace** says 2027 in the title and 2026 in the body.
- "Product Designer, New Grad" at **Palantir** is the most junior-sounding title on the internet and excludes you outright.

So: pull the descriptions, grep for `years`, grep for a graduation year, and rank by what the
posting actually asks for. That is a fifteen-minute pass over a hundred roles, and it is the
difference between eight real applications and eighty wasted ones.
