No quarterback before round eight. Not a preference — a margin. On this year's projections the best quarterback in football is worth +4.8 pts/gm over the one still sitting on the board in round thirteen. The best running back is worth +13.4 over his. Last season Drake Maye finished QB2 at 21.1 pts/gm and Trevor Lawrence QB5 at 19.9, both from well outside the early rounds. Where a big name's ADP lands inside a round below, the round note says plainly why we are passing.

Running back at every slot, not just the good ones. Backs decay roughly twice as fast as receivers: RB13–RB24 average 13.6 pts/gm and RB25–RB36 average 10.5, a 3.1-point step, where the same move at receiver costs 1.8. The model weights running back 1.20× and holds a hard floor — two backs by round four, three by six, four by eight — at all twelve slots.

Nothing here is a reach. Every pick is checked against Fantasy Football Calculator's 12-team PPR ADP, taken from 7,830 mock drafts between 19 and 26 August. In rounds one to six no recommendation is more than half a round ahead of its ADP; later, where a reach costs nothing, the number of picks you are jumping is printed on the row.

Where this comes from

Projections, ceilings and floors: WalterPicks 2026 redraft PPR rankings, 26 August 2026 — the same file every points-per-game, ceiling and floor number on this page is read from. ADP: Fantasy Football Calculator 12-team PPR, 7,830 mock drafts, 19–26 August 2026, with Underdog best-ball ADP as the second market read. Both are printed on every row so you can see where the two markets disagree.

How the picks are chosen. Eleven simulated opponents draft strictly by ADP. At each of your picks the model scores what is left on value over replacement — RB38, WR34, QB13, TE13 for this roster — weights running back 1.20×, adds an urgency term for any positional floor still unmet, refuses a pick more than half a round ahead of ADP in rounds one to six, and from round ten onward pays for the gap between a player's projection and his ceiling rather than for his floor. From round eleven it prefers a player with an actual report behind him over one with only a projection.

On the notes. A note marked report or stat is something written on the page it links to; open the link and read it. A note marked projection only is derived from the WalterPicks numbers and nothing else — there is no camp report behind it and it is not pretending there is. Camp news moves fast: check the injury flags against your own beat writers on draft day.
