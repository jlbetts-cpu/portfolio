Overview

Cluster is a campus app that helps students find clubs they would never stumble on. My first full design project: five weeks, a brand-new tool, a team of three. It is the one that made me fall in love with product design.

Problem

More than 800 clubs, and no way to find them.

UC Davis is home to more than 800 student clubs, but the ones that are not already big are basically invisible, buried under thin advertising and impossible to find unless you catch the once-a-year club fair. I felt it firsthand: a transfer that quarter, new to campus and looking for my people, with no idea where to start. Every transfer I talked to said the same thing, no clue how many clubs existed, or how to find the niche ones they would actually love.

The build

A first real project, learned in five weeks.

I came to Davis as a communications major. The moment I found out the school offered design I was hooked, even though I did not yet know what UI, UX, or product design meant, it just looked neat and I wanted to try. After a few clubs turned out to have a barrier of entry my skills could not clear, I found Design Interactive, a club with a fellowship built for total beginners. I spent five weeks designing every screen of Cluster while teaching myself Figma from zero, showing my roommate something I had tweaked each day to hear what he thought. I did not want to be good, I wanted to be great. My title was product designer, but I took the lead the whole way: if I did not step up, I was not sure anyone would, and stepping up is something I love.

The turning point

The visit that made it click.

The moment I knew this was what I wanted was visiting LinkedIn’s headquarters. I could picture myself working somewhere like that one day, the calm, the kindness, being around my design peers. I carpooled the club’s presidents on the drive, and by the end of the program they were telling me to apply for board. I did, and got in. Cluster showed me how much I pour into something the second I am in, and design was the perfect way to channel it.

The concept

A home where every club can be found.

Cluster gives every club a place to show up: they publish articles and events, and you can heart, follow, and actually attend. The Discover feed is catered to your interests, but sometimes you would rather browse by mood, so Sports, Dance, Design, and more each get their own feed. Club profiles gather a club’s articles and events in one place, so you feel what being a member is really like. And messaging makes it social: message a club you want to join, or send a friend an event they would love, because finding your people should feel like college, not a database.

Outcome

Most Innovative, and the reason design stuck.

Our team won Most Innovative. I was thrilled, I am competitive to my core, and I have carried that hunger into every project since. Looking back from 2026, there is plenty I would change: cut the messaging, tighten the spacing and typography, lean on the kind of design system I now use religiously (Cluster had none, I did not know where to start yet). But I still see the effort in every screen.

There is a funny footnote, too. In 2026 a friend shipped an app called tapin that does almost exactly what Cluster set out to do, and I helped him polish the UI over coffee at the CoHo, my name is even in one of its App Store screenshots. Cluster never shipped, but it is the reason I went all in on design. The chip on my shoulder from it is one I never want to lose, it keeps me hungry to keep learning, and that same drive runs straight through Bearings, Apollo, and Strata.
