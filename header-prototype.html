The site header — one component, five page types, two widths

The bar is the lockup plus three items: Home · About · Games. Contact has left it. The lockup is back to two stacked lines, still sentence case — see §5 for the four ways to solve the width mismatch and why three of them distort the type. The socials sit in a row above the bar on the home page and slide into the bar as that row scrolls away. The availability line is a hero element above the <h1>, not header furniture. The four moods have left the header entirely for a dock under the head they act on.

One component, every page. The specimen recommended a pill on home and a ledge on case studies; that is the current bug wearing better paint — index.html and the five case studies already run two different headers. The only per-page differences here are a back arrow, which item is lit, and one attribute for a dark ground.

Everything is live. Click the dock, click About, scroll the frames, tab through them. Every 390px view is a real 390px viewport, not a resized window.

1 · Home — at rest, and after one scroll

Scroll the left frame. The social row leaves and does not come back; the bar sticks; the glyphs slide into the bar's trailing slot on --sp-quick. Contact is never off screen and never costs a second row.

Home· data-nav="home" · social row + sticky bar

Home stays lit at the top and scrolled down among the case studies — the work lives on Home, so there is nothing else for the scroll position to mean. That is why there is no fourth "Work" slot. The capsule above the headline is the hero's, not the header's: it is .heroAvail restyled, in its existing position, keeping its existing entrance.

Home, About open· data-nav="about" · an overlay, not a route

About is a state, not a destination. It is a <button aria-expanded> that toggles the existing takeover, so it takes aria-current="true" (this is the current thing) rather than aria-current="page" (this URL). The lockup grows its back arrow while the overlay is open — which is what retires .heroBack and .navTitle. Known cost: About still has no shareable URL.

2 · The mood dock — under the head, on the grid

The four moods were sitting in the site header, acting on something 400px below it. They are now a child of the hero grid's centre column, directly under the head — not an absolutely-positioned corner element, which is what .faceMoodCorner (right:-14px) is today. Click it, then pick a mood. Nothing was collapsed and nothing was reordered: the four moods, the separator, Add your head, Add an egghead, the saved-heads grid and Show on home are in the order they ship in.

Dock open· moods first, roster below the separator

Where the roster went. Games is a page now, so the Play menu no longer exists to hold anything. The moods went where Jayden asked. Add your head, Add an egghead, the saved-heads grid and Show on home came with them, below a separator, because they act on the same object: the heads on this screen. They are head controls, not site navigation — the header was the wrong owner for both halves.

The trigger states the current mood rather than saying "Play": a control and a readout in one slot, so you can see the mood without opening anything.

One collision to resolve on adoption. .scrollCue sits at bottom:18px in the hero and the dock takes that space. The cue moves below the dock or goes — listed in the report.

3 · The other three page types

No social row on a sub-page — one bar, 48px, glyphs already inside it. The row is a home-page letterhead; on a case study it would be a second layer between a visitor and the work.

Case study· data-nav="home" · chapter rail at left

The rail is sticky under the bar (top: calc(var(--nav-h) + 26px)). Scroll the frame: nothing collides, and the bar's shadow deepens once content passes beneath it — the one and only thing that changes on scroll.

play.html· data-nav="games" · paper, fixed bar

Games is a real destination now, so it lights like one (aria-current="page"). The arena is full-viewport and must not scroll during a match, so the bar is fixed, not sticky — same object, same geometry. Its wrapper is pointer-events:none with the bar opting back in, so it never eats a tap meant for the pitch.

gradientlab.html· data-surface="ink" · data-nav unset

Nothing is lit, and that is the answer. The Lab is noindex and is not in the nav, so forcing an active item would be a lie. Dark glass rather than a white pill because the stage colour is whatever the user dials in — white ink on --mat-i3 clears 4.5:1 over a white ground and over near-black. The existing pointer-events:none header contract is preserved exactly.

Real 390px viewports. Three items and the lockup fit inline, which means .navBurger, aside.navDrawer, .ndLink and .navScrim can be deleted outright — a whole mobile component retired, not restyled. Numbers below are read live off these frames.

5 · The lockup — what was measured

measuring…

Two stacked lines, sentence case, ragged right, flush left. Instrument Sans 600, both lines at one size and one tracking, --tr-mark. No monogram, no glyph, no drawn mark, present at 390px on every page.

The rag is the design, not a compromise. Width-matching sentence case needs wdth 76.81% against an axis floor of 75 — 1.8pp of headroom, and lowercase bowls and stems distort long before that. Per-line tracking needs +0.19em on "Betts", two textures in one mark. Two optical sizes needs line 2 at 22.1px, which reads as surname emphasis. Holding the texture constant and letting the width fall where it falls is the only one of the four that does not distort something.

It is 45% narrower and 95% taller than the single line — 54.9 × 30.7px against 100.1 × 15.7px. That is the presence, and the narrowing is what paid for the mail glyph in the 390px bar.

Where it sits: leading the bar, and the bar centred on the page. It is the back affordance on every sub-page, and a back control belongs at the leading edge. The five case studies centre it today in a 1fr auto 1fr grid — that is exactly what leaves three children stranded at three unrelated x-positions with nothing binding them.

Optical, not metric. Two corrections, both derived rather than nudged: a baseline correction for mixing 17px and 14px in one row, and a 1px tighter leading inset measured off the sidebearings. Both are in the numbers on the left.

Four states, not two. Rest, hover, :focus-visible (2px accent ring, 2px offset), and :active — scale(.97) on items, scale(.985) on the lockup, because a bigger object needs less scale to read as the same press.

6 · What earned a slot, and what did not

Kept
The lockup, stacked, at every width, doubling as Back on sub-pages and while the About overlay is open — one slot, two jobs, a real 44 × 44 target.
Home · About · Games, the three named.
The socials, in the row on home and inside the bar once that row has gone — contact on every page at zero vertical cost, phones included, which the single-line lockup could not afford.

Cut

3. A separate Back slot. Merged into the lockup.

7. The footer availability line. It moves into the hero capsule.
8. No search. Five case studies is not a corpus.

7 · Two things to decide

1Home and the lockup go to the same place. Two leading slots, one destination. It is defensible — the lockup is brand-and-back, Home is the you-are-here indicator, and something has to light up. If you want the bar tighter, the lockup can absorb Home and the bar drops to two items. Five-minute change either way.

2Availability now exists in exactly one place. It comes out of the footer and lives in the home hero, so a recruiter who lands straight on a case study never sees it. Cheapest fix if you want one: the same capsule above each case-study <h1>. It is the same component and costs one line of markup per page.
