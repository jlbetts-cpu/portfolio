About

Improv as a way to grow, not a way to perform.

Developmental improvisation is the work of Linda Kellogg Fulton. It takes the games that improvisers use to get funny and uses them to get brave.

What developmental improvisation is

Stage improv is about the audience. Developmental improv is about the person playing. The exercises look similar from the outside, but the point of each one is what it builds in the player: the nerve to speak first, the patience to listen, the memory to hold a scene together, the trust to let somebody else lead.

It is social-emotional learning from the inside out. Nobody is told to be confident. They play a game in which the only way through is to be, and then they notice that they were.

The games are organised as the Fulton Method, in three phases that take a group from first laughs to running scenes on their own. Teachers, coaches, medical professionals and parents can learn to run it themselves through the train-the-trainer program.

What it builds

  • Self-confidence
  • Cooperation
  • Focus
  • Listening
  • Memory recall
  • Communication
  • Intuition
  • Problem solving
  • Spontaneity
  • Trust
  • Self-validation
  • Support of others

Who Linda is

Linda Kellogg Fulton has taught improvisation for more than fifty years. She found it at fourteen, in 1973, as a kid with severe dyslexia who had decided she had no value and no purpose. Improv gave her both back, and she has spent the rest of her life passing that on.

She is the founder of Total Improv Kids, an award-winning program that has taught thousands of children in Los Angeles, and the author of [book title to confirm: “The Power of Developmental Improvisation”]. She speaks about developmental improvisation around the country and the world, and trains teachers, coaches and therapists in the Fulton Method.
